P5	Heading 1	1. What this suite is for
P6	Normal	The six Level 1 reports are the standing executive monitors for running a community bank or credit union against a published plan. They are a Kaplan–Norton balanced scorecard at board and Executive Leadership Team altitude: objectives first, one proving metric per objective, Actual versus Target versus Peer, a named owner, and a three-step drill that ends in a named action.
P7	Normal	Level 1 means board and president altitude only. These pages answer three questions in a meeting: Are we on plan this period? Why? What are we doing about it? They are not a general-ledger close package, not a branch league table, and not a full planning workspace. Add and Edit on the top bar bind a sample objective in this browser session only (see section 3).
P8	Normal	The Cause chain is built to show
P9	Normal	People (Level 1-6) → Process, service and production (Level 1-5) → Customer / member franchise (Level 1-3) → Financial results (Level 1-2). Balance sheet, risk and capital (Level 1-4) is a constraint rail across that chain, not a fifth earnings box. Level 1-1 is the enterprise map and the only page the board pack needs if time is short.
P10	Normal	Bank versus credit union
P11	Normal	One framework, two label sets. The Bank / Credit union toggle on every page relabels metrics (deposits versus shares, Loans-to-Deposits versus Loans-to-Shares, products per household versus Average Member Relationship, Return on Equity versus net worth growth). It does not invent parallel math. Peers stay inside charter: Uniform Bank Performance Report for banks, Financial Performance Report / 5300 Call Report for credit unions. Never mix those two peer books on the same curve. Riverbend Community Bank is the first live sample; credit-union mode is a demo label set until a second workshop.
P12	Heading 1	2. What you will see on first open
P13	Normal	Open any of the six L1 report files. They share one engine, so navigation, period pills, and the drill pane behave the same way.
P14	List Bullet	Top bar: institution name, Bank / Credit union toggle, four page pills (Monitor, Add, Edit, Definitions), links out of the suite.
P15	List Bullet	Report nav: Level 1-1 Strategy through Level 1-6 People. Switching reports stays in the same browser window.
P16	List Bullet	Period pills on every report: Month (default, July 2026), Quarter (last closed quarter, second quarter 2026 average), Year-to-date (running mean through July), versus prior year (this year overlaid on last year).
P17	List Bullet	Needs attention: enterprise exceptions (off track / at risk versus the published target), plus a Branch & associate · Level 2 exceptions strip where that grain exists.
P18	List Bullet	Strategy map (Level 1-1): click a perspective box to open that report. Risk sits beside the chain as a constraint.
P19	List Bullet	Objective tiles: Actual, Your target, versus goal (or versus last year when that period is on), peer chip, sparkline, status badge.
P20	List Bullet	Right-hand drill, three tabs only: 1 · Key performance indicator & gaps; 2 · Trend & peers; 3 · Drivers & action. There is no fourth drill.
P21	List Bullet	Definitions mode: the same metrics as a table — Metric, Perspective, CAMELS letter, Definition, Formula, Source.
P22	Normal	Status on every tile is Actual versus the institution’s own target (goal, limit, band, or floor). Peers pressure-test the plan; they do not replace it. Green / amber / red is not a Uniform Bank Performance Report grade.
P23	Heading 1	3. Add and Edit metric objectives
P24	Normal	The top bar now has four pills, not two. Monitor is the standing executive session. Definitions is the formula lock. Add and Edit let a presenter bind or change a proving metric without leaving the six pages. They are session-only demo controls — not a live planning workspace, not calculation quality assurance, and not a write to a warehouse.
P25	Normal	Add — new metric objective. Opens a form on the report you are on. Required fields are metric name, the objective in words, and a numeric target. Optional fields are owner (defaults to the report owner), peer (defaults to a sample offset from target), current actual (otherwise the mockup generates a six-month approach-to-target series), unit, and direction (higher is better, lower is better, or stay inside a band). Create & monitor returns to Monitor and opens the new tile so status, gaps, and the sparkline are visible immediately.
P26	List Bullet	On Level 1-1, pick the strategy-map home (Financial, Customer / Member, Risk / Capital, Process / Production, or People) so the map bar counts the new tile. On Level 1-2 through Level 1-6, a checkbox can also pin the tile on Level 1-1. On Level 1-5, pick Operations or Production so the card lands in the right stack.
P27	Normal	Edit — existing metric objective. Opens the same form against a metric already on the report. The picker defaults to the tile that was selected on Monitor. Changes apply to the active charter side only (Bank versus Credit union), so Return on Equity and net worth growth stay independent. You can change name, objective, owner, target, peer, unit, and direction (including a band). Save changes returns to Monitor on that tile. Badge, gaps, and the sparkline recompute from the new target in the same session.
P28	Normal	Refresh the browser and the sample catalog is restored. A session-added tile has a Definitions stub (formula “to be determined — session add”). In a live institution, earnings and franchise target changes still go through Asset-Liability Committee; risk limits and capital floors still go through the board.

P43	Heading 1	6. Purpose of each dashboard
P44	Normal	Level 1-1 — Enterprise Strategy Scorecard
P45	Normal	This page is the only report the board needs if the meeting is short. Owner in the mockup: Executive Leadership Team (Chief Executive Officer chairs).
P46	Normal	Decision is built to force: Are we executing the plan as a leadership team, and which objective needs a course correction this period?
P47	Normal	Nine proving tiles (this year’s board pack): Return on Assets; Efficiency ratio; Tier 1 leverage / Net worth; Customer Satisfaction Index / Net Promoter Score; Retention; Loan growth; Core funding mix; Past due 30 days or more; Engagement. Each tile is an objective with one number — not a catalog of every metric in the institution.
P48	Normal	The strategy map on this page is the cause chain. Clicking a box opens the owning Level 1 report. Bank caption: Learning and Growth → Process → Customer → Financial (shareholder value). Risk constrains. Credit-union caption: Learning and Growth → Process → Member → Financial (member value + net worth). Risk constrains. Sample causal lines in the mockup: bank, credit quality → provision → Return on Assets; credit union, efficiency → capital retention → net worth.
P49	Normal	Use this page to start and to end the conversation. Do not turn it into six metric walls. If a tile is off track, go to the owning report and stop at the named action.
P50	Normal	Level 1-2 — Financial Performance and Earnings
P51	Normal	Owner: Chief Financial Officer. This is the earnings quality and growth scorecard — whether volume is producing acceptable, sustainable returns.
P52	Normal	Decision: Is growth producing acceptable, sustainable returns — and where do we reprice, cut, or invest?
P53	Normal	Bank story: earn competitive Return on Assets / Return on Equity without stretching credit or wholesale funding. Credit-union story: earn enough Return on Assets to fund net-worth growth and member value. Capital generation (Return on Equity or net worth growth) is scored here. Capital adequacy (Tier 1 leverage or net worth ratio) lives on Level 1-4 and is echoed on Level 1-1.
P54	Normal	Cause lines the page is meant to make visible: core funding mix (Level 1-3) → cost of funds → Net Interest Margin → net interest income; products / Average Member Relationship (Level 1-3) → fee income → noninterest mix; process efficiency (Level 1-5) → expense → efficiency ratio; credit quality (Level 1-4) → provision → net income → Return on Assets.
P55	Normal	Net Interest Margin is scored here as earnings quality. Interest-rate-risk limits and the shock path are managed on Level 1-4. Efficiency is scored here and explained on Level 1-5. That split is intentional — it is not a missing process metric.
P56	Normal	Level 1-3 — Customer / Member Franchise
P57	Normal	Owner: Chief Marketing Officer, as franchise owner — not as a marketing-mix owner. This page measures whether the institution is deepening the relationships that fund it, or buying volume.
P58	Normal	Decision: Franchise value — growth, engagement, and depth. Are we deepening the relationships that fund the institution, or buying volume?
P59	Normal	Banks measure household franchise depth (Customer Satisfaction Index, household retention, products per household, core deposit mix). Credit unions measure member-owner depth (Net Promoter Score, member retention, Average Member Relationship, core share mix). The math is the same spine; the labels change. Customer Acquisition Cost is here so “growth” cannot hide purchased households.
P60	Normal	Digital active on this page is franchise adoption (eligible customers or members with a 90-day login). Digital self-service on Level 1-5 is an operations mix (eligible transactions completed without a banker). Different denominators, labeled on purpose.
P61	Normal	There is no deposit pipeline tile. Deposits show up as growth, mix, Loans-to-Deposits / Loans-to-Shares, and wholesale. There is no seventh Sales report. Production conversion lives on Level 1-5.
P62	Normal	Level 1-4 — Balance Sheet, Risk and Capital
P63	Normal	Owner: Chief Risk Officer. This is the constraint rail: whether risk is accumulating faster than capital, liquidity, and controls can absorb. It is first-class in the suite and is not a seventh strategy or a second examination manual.
P64	Normal	Decision: Is risk accumulating faster than capital, liquidity, and controls can absorb?
P65	Normal	The page is organized the way an Asset-Liability Committee / risk committee thinks, even though the mockup is a single grid: capital adequacy; credit (past due, criticized, Allowance for Credit Losses); liquidity utilization (Loans-to-Deposits or Loans-to-Shares, wholesale); interest-rate-risk net-interest-income shock; Bank Secrecy Act aging as a Management control key risk indicator.
P66	Normal	CAMELS letters appear on these tiles (see section 7). They tag which examination component the metric informs. They do not weight the score. Status remains Actual versus the board / Asset-Liability Committee target, limit, band, or floor.
P67	Normal	Enterprise ratios on this page stay enterprise-only. There is no branch grain on capital, Loans-to-Deposits, interest-rate risk, or Bank Secrecy Act aging. A full Asset-Liability Management book, Bank Secrecy Act inventory, and cyber security-operations center are out of Level 1 by design.
P68	Normal	Level 1-5 — Process, Service and Production
P69	Normal	Dual-owned: Chief Operating Officer (run the book) and Chief Business Officer (convert the plan). The mockup splits the cards into Operations and Production so the meeting does not mix service-level agreement with pipeline.
P70	Normal	Decision: Can operations run the book at acceptable cost and speed — and is production converting the plan?
P71	Normal	Operations (Chief Operating Officer): decision cycle, cost per transaction, digital self-service, teller service-level agreement, and — in credit-union mode — member onboarding cycle. Production (Chief Business Officer): funnel conversion, yield on new originations, origination volume, commercial pipeline (bank). Loan growth and deposit growth are outcomes and stay on Level 1-2.
P72	Normal	This is where “growth and pipeline” content belongs as conversion, not as stock. Commercial pipeline percent is the loan pipeline tile. There is no deposit pipeline tile. Total loans, total deposits, and loans minus deposits are not tiles; closest context is loan growth, deposit growth, Loans-to-Deposits / Loans-to-Shares, and wholesale.
P73	Normal	Level 1-6 — People and Organizational Capacity
P74	Normal	Owner: Chief Human Resources Officer. A green earnings page with a hollow bench is a lagging failure. This page asks whether the institution has the people to run the strategy safely for the next 12–24 months.
P75	Normal	Decision: Do we have the people to run the strategy safely for the next 12–24 months?
P76	Normal	Engagement and frontline turnover are leading indicators for Customer Satisfaction Index and teller service-level agreement. Training and commercial credentials feed pipeline conversion on Level 1-5. Succession on examiner-sensitive seats (Bank Secrecy Act, credit, Chief Financial Officer minus one) feeds the control environment on Level 1-4. Credit-union mode swaps commercial skill coverage for volunteer board education hours — a Management / governance metric, not a bank credential count.
P77	Normal	Bank Secrecy Act inventory and cyber operations do not live here. Those controls sit on Level 1-4. Time-to-fill on this page is the people constraint that makes Bank Secrecy Act aging worse.
P78	Heading 1	7. How CAMELS is used
P79	Normal	CAMELS is the federal examination rating system: Capital, Asset quality, Management, Earnings, Liquidity, and Sensitivity to market risk. Examiners assign those component ratings to the institution. They do not grade this balanced scorecard.
P80	Normal	The suite uses CAMELS as a tag, not as a weight and not as six extra reports. Every proving metric in the catalog carries one letter so leadership can see which examination component a number informs. The tile’s status (on track / at risk / off track) is still Actual versus the published target. A “C” on capital does not make capital more important than Return on Assets in the score. A “C” only says: this number is how we talk about Capital.
P81	Normal	
P82	Normal	Where the letter is visible in the mockups
P83	List Bullet	Definitions tab on every report: a CAMELS column. Caption states that letters are tags, not weights.
P84	List Bullet	Level 1-4 tiles: a letter chip on each card (Capital, Asset quality, Liquidity, Sensitivity, Management).
P85	List Bullet	Level 1-1: letter chips only on the two risk tiles that sit on the enterprise page — capital (C) and past due (A). Other Level 1-1 tiles do not show a chip, so the board page does not look like an examination scorecard.
P86	List Bullet	Level 1-2, Level 1-3, Level 1-5, and Level 1-6 cards do not show the chip. The letter is still in Definitions if someone asks.
P87	Normal	Level 1-4 is the CAMELS rail: Capital, Asset quality, Liquidity, Sensitivity, plus Management control key risk indicators (Bank Secrecy Act aging). The suite does not create a Capital report, an Asset-quality report, a Management report, and so on. That would recreate the examination manual and bury the strategy conversation.
P88	Normal	Talking point if a director asks “what is our CAMELS?”: this product does not display or predict the confidential supervisory rating. It shows the operating metrics the board already owns that examiners will also ask about, tagged so the conversation can move from strategy to safety and soundness without changing pages six times.
P89	Heading 1	8. How to read a tile and the three drills
P90	Normal	On the tile
P91	List Bullet	Badge: On track, At risk, or Off track versus the institution target. Direction matters: higher-is-better, lower-is-better, or stay inside a band (Loans-to-Deposits / Loans-to-Shares).
P92	List Bullet	Actual / Your target / versus goal — or versus last year when that period pill is selected. The badge and the progress bar remain versus target even in versus-prior-year mode.
P93	List Bullet	Peer chip: ahead of or behind the same-charter, same-size median. Uniform Bank Performance Report $1 billion–$3 billion for the bank; Financial Performance Report for the credit-union demo.
P94	List Bullet	Sparkline: this year’s path and a dotted target. When versus prior year is on, last year is a gray solid line (not gold). Gold is reserved for peer.
P95	Normal	Tab 1 · Key performance indicator & gaps
P96	Normal	The gap story, not another chart of the same three levels. Target type (Goal, Limit, Band, or Floor) sits above three cards: versus plan, versus peer, versus last year. A one-line Close the gap states what it would take to get back to plan (financial metrics also note that five months remain in 2026 from the July as-of). Interest Rate Risk distinguishes the −8% Asset-Liability Committee floor from the −6% plan.
P97	Normal	Tab 2 · Trend & peers
P98	Normal	A larger trend (actual, target, and either peer median or last year) plus a short named-peer table. Peer names are sample institutions in-charter. Curves never mix Uniform Bank Performance Report and Financial Performance Report.
P99	Normal	Tab 3 · Drivers & action
P100	Normal	Two or three named drivers (labels, not a click-path into child metrics) and one named action with owner and due date. Where the metric has Level 2 grain, two small tables appear: four sample branches and one or two associates to coach. Caption in the product: sample Level 2 grain — not a seventh Level 1 report or a full roster. Board conversation stops here.
P101	Normal	There is no department grain in these mockups. Branch and associate detail is a demonstration that the enterprise miss can be located, not a production coaching system. A live Level 2 product would be a later phase.
